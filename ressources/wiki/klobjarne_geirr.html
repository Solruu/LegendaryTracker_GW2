Klobjarne Geirr is a legendary spear with six cosmetic styles. All six styles are unlocked as skins upon binding Klobjarne Geirr to the player's account. Each of the six styles of the spear represent the Southward Claw and their five closest allies from an ancient kodan tale.

Lore[edit]

Klobjarne Geirr
As the storm raged outside, the Claw looked over his people. Bearkin were huddled together for warmth—their bellies rumbled, and worry laced their eyes. But the wind was too fierce and the falling snow too thick to hunt. He looked at shivering cubs and made his choice: the Claw would lead them south, away from danger. He could wait for her no longer.

Graphics[edit]

Klobjarne Geirr is a lowland kodan theme spear imbued with a ghostly-spirit aura. The weapon visually evolves based on the skill being used, similar to how the Exordium changes shapes, each type of skill uses a different ghostly spearhead from behind the user.

Draw/Stow effects
  • When drawing, the spear glows intensely with the bluish-ghostly aura while the other five spear styles materialize as translucent, ghostly forms that float behind the user.
  • When drawn, the spear pulses with an initial bluish-ghostly aura, followed by the orangish shiny light emitted by the bronze inscription on the spearhead.
  • When stowing, the ghostly spearheads merge back into the main weapon in a spiraling motion, leaving a faint wisp of spectral energy behind.
  • When stowed, the orangish shiny light continues to emit from the bronze inscription on the spearhead, while the bluish ghostly aura ceases pulsing.
Footfalls
  • User leaves behinds swirls of mist.
Killing blow animation
  • Enemies killed by a critical hit will trigger a visual effect: a large, translucent version of the current Klobjarne skin appears, impaling the enemy, followed by the other five spearheads materializing around the spear, each delivering a secondary impaling strike.
Skill effects
  • During melee chain attacks, the spearhead will swap between three set spears from behind your back with the translucent spirit aura.
  • Projectile attacks will throw a matching visual for user choice of spear, as well as all of the ghost spear heads as a chasing wave.
  • Skill effects are tied to the spirit spearhead from the arsenal positioned behind the user's back. If using a skin variant that: not Gierr, Gierr will take the position of that skin in the arsenal.
    • The arsenal order behind the user's back follows a fixed arrangement from left to right: Hler, Kesja, Darr, Frakka, Atgeir. The Klobjarne Geirr: the only spearhead whose position changes.
    • For example, if changing the skin from Geirr to Frakka, Geirr will occupy the Frakka position, while the other skins maintain their original positions.

Notes[edit]

  • The default skin is Klobjarne Geirr. Upon adding the weapon to the Legendary Armory, the skins Klobjarne Atgeir, Klobjarne Darr, Klobjarne Kesja, Klobjarne Frakka, and Klobjarne Hler are all unlocked as well.
  • The floating spears behind the user will be the other unlocked skins of the Klobjarne Geirr not currently being used. Changing to another of the Klobjarne Geirr skins will change floating spear set.
Bug:
When equipped as an underwater weapon or when Klobjarne Geirr is the inactive weapon, the killing blow animation can still trigger.

Trivia[edit]

  • Klobjarne was released with the first content update for Guild Wars 2: Janthir Wilds, Godspawn.[1]
  • The name Klobjarne is most likely derived from Old Norse kló and bjǫrn, which mean "claw" and "bear", respectively.
  • Its variant names generally refer to different spears or polearm weapons:
    • Geirr is Old Norse for "spear".[2]
    • Atgeir and kesja refer to a glaive or halberd. Atgeir is a derivative of geirr above, and kesja is a loanword from the Proto-Celtic gaisos / Celtic-Latin gaesum for "javelin".[3][4]
    • Darr loosely means "javelin", sharing the same root with the word "dart".
    • Frakka refers to a spear or lance. This word is where the Frank people derive their name from.
    • Hler, shaped like a trident, is the only one that does not directly refer to a polearm weapon. Hlér is the Old Norse name for the sea.[5]
